At Codedevza AI and Wastify AI, we are a small team. High-performing, lean, and hungry. I know what we can do and I know what is not in our power. Those two things are equally important to understand.

Over the past few months, I've watched AI reshape how we work. I can see where this is going in the coming weeks, months, and years. I've been telling my partner to shift our core focus from development to sales. He always asks the same question: "Who will handle it, Usama?" I always reply the same way: "I will do it all".

The truth is, AI has changed what "all" means. It has increased our appetite for work in ways I couldn't have predicted even six months ago.

## The software people
Tech people are the first to get exposed to what's new in AI. Or that was the case a few weeks ago, at least. I use X a lot and it's my primary source of tech news.

I saw the inorganic push of Clawdbot (OpenClaw) in front of my eyes. I do believe there's a larger force beyond my knowledge that decides what gets "fame" and what doesn't. Some things are of actual value and deserve it though. OpenClaw was one of those things. I was one of the first to set it up on my Macbook and I immediately connected my WhatsApp to it. It was amazing to control my Macbook by talking through WhatsApp. Who wouldn't want that?

I did delete it shortly afterwards. It is supposed to be "local" and hence "safe" if used with a self-hosted model. Maybe. Maybe not. We have seen how successful open-source supply chain attacks are and there's not much we can do about it. The backdoor in XZ Utils. The LiteLLM vulnerability. The Axios attack. It is that easy for an ill-intentioned maintainer to push something malicious into millions of machines. These were just the instances that got uncovered. A person like me could almost pull it off and I'm not even an expert.

The reason software people were at the forefront until now was because AI was directly benefiting us. I was THE beneficiary of better models. Jack Dorsey tweeted "everything is programming". Elon replied "Not hardware". Jack replied, "Not today". 

## Vibe coding
I burned 1B tokens in Cursor in 2025. Since the start of 2026, my usage is near a billion tokens every month, if not more. Software development has become easy.

Before someone says "Vibe Coding" ruins software: no, it doesn't. I vibe code with my brain open and millions of records of data are created monthly in our in-house products. We have systems working in production used indirectly by the likes of Google, Duolingo, big banks and what not. 

## Event-driven AI
Event-driven AI was the one thing we were missing the most.

If I look at myself and most humans, most of our actions are responses to external stimuli. Even actions that don't appear externally triggered are, in fact, delayed reactions layered deep in our thoughts. Newton's third law at work. The stimulus could be an email, a notification, a snap, or even the "thought" to open Instagram.

Most of my use of AI had been in writing software where the model responded to my prompts and that was it. Then came the `/loop` command in Claude Code. I could leave my Mac running the whole night and wake up to something new. Something I didn't prompt. That was the moment where the possibilities became endless.

A few months back, I learned about the browser-use repository being funded by YC. It caught my attention and led me to join their Discord. After browsing their #showcase channel, I could immediately tell where we were heading. Then came macos-use, and soon we could store repetitive workflows. The core principle in use there was simple: take screenshots, and the LLM returns coordinates of where to click and where to type.

## The Matrix Profile
OpenClaw, Perplexity Computer, Claude Cowork. We have heard enough about all of these. Using Cowork at Codedevza, we were able to run scheduled sessions of Opus 4.6 opening our browser, looking for work, applying for work, and even scheduling calls for us. I am not overstating it or glorifying it. I am actually understating it.

Cowork could not only do all this, but run a self-improving loop where we allowed it to edit the initial playbook. It started learning patterns in human behaviour that I used to teach to others manually. It started profiling clients using variables like minimum spend, platform age, and more. There are things we use it for that I can't disclose just yet, and it extends far beyond sales.

We have never gotten a single dollar of business from Upwork. It was one of the sales channels I had planned to be on. I was building the "Ultimate" Upwork extension to automate most of the process. I am a big believer in consistency beating EVERYTHING else. But before I could finish building it, my partner started experimenting with Cowork and it made much of what I was building obsolete.

After witnessing these capabilities first hand, my first thought was to design an architecture where agents could work in separate environments, each with a personality and role. No, I cannot afford a Mac Mini for each agent. I spun up Docker containers instead, each with its own browser and apps, to prevent inter-agent collisions. I can already see myself having a team of agents doing independently what I never had the time to do.

An endless pit of opportunity has opened before us. I could spend months on this and still have room for more. Not using these tools is not a choice anymore.

For that, I have decided to create what I call a Matrix Profile: a decision about what personal data I am willing to share with the Matrix. I expect everyone will eventually need to do the same.

I was surprised to learn that Composer 2 by Cursor was using Kimi 2.5, a model by Moonshot trained with reinforcement learning. This was never explicitly revealed. It was only added to Cursor's documentation after someone on X uncovered it.

Claude Code's source was leaked and people built open-source versions of it. You could self-host Kimi 2.5 and burn tokens for free forever. But at this point, we can't afford to use an underperforming model. What Cursor provides in a $200 subscription isn't just model performance. It's token usage worth over $600 for top models like Opus 4.6 and GPT 5.4. Cheaper models aren't an option when your output depends on it.

Nikita Bier is vibe coding and pushing X updates quicker than Apple can review the builds. The people who win will be the ones who figure out what humans are still needed for. At Codedevza, we are already thinking about this. At Wastify, we have hardware at the core of our product. Hardware still needs hands. We expect to last and we plan to build for the future.
