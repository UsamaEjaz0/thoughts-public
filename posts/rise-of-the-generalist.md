If you are wondering if your job will be replaced by AI, then this article is for you. We have already seen AI finish a week of our work in a few minutes.


## The numbers, so far
The cleanest data on this comes from inside Anthropic. In March it [published](https://www.anthropic.com/research/labor-market-impacts) a study mapping a million Claude conversations against the U.S. occupational database, asking, for each profession, how much of the day-to-day work the AI was already doing.

![Top ten most AI-exposed occupations by share of tasks already done by AI, from Anthropic's Labor Market Impacts report. Computer programmers lead at 74.5%, followed by customer service representatives at 70.1% and data entry keyers at 67.1%.](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/UsamaEjaz0/thoughts-public/main/images/most-ai-exposed-occupations.png)

The fields with effectively zero exposure were the physical ones. Cooks, electricians, plumbers, carers, lifeguards. Anything that lives in a screen is in AI's coverage. Anything that lives in a hand and a room is not, for the moment at least.

The same study also compared what AI can theoretically do against what it is actually being used for at work. Across most office work, the gap is still wide.

![Radar chart from Anthropic comparing theoretical AI capability (blue) against observed AI usage (red) across occupational categories. The blue area extends far beyond the red across every white-collar category, with the largest gaps in business and finance, computer and math, and legal work; physical categories like construction, agriculture, and personal care show low values on both measures.](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/UsamaEjaz0/thoughts-public/main/images/ai-coverage.png)

Blue is what AI can already do. Red is what companies are actually letting it do today. The gap between them is the rest of the change, still on the way. Most large companies are slow. They are still buying the tools and hooking them into their systems. Once those decisions land, red catches up to blue, and the job market shifts again.

Since ChatGPT released in late 2022, we are noticing another statistic. Hiring of 22-to-25-year-olds into the most AI-exposed professions has fallen [14%](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/economics/2026/0224) but the wages are not falling. Let me re-iterate. Wages in AI-exposed professions are not falling!

Why? A few reasons:

- **Companies cut hiring, not pay.** It is easier for a firm to freeze new hires than to cut the salaries of people already on the team. The one taking the shock is whoever was supposed to walk in the door next.

- **AI replaces codified knowledge, not tacit.** Codified knowledge is the kind that lives in textbooks and procedures. Tacit knowledge is what experienced workers earn by doing the job for ten years. AI is good at the first and not so good at the second.

- **Juniors' value was mostly codified. Seniors' value is mostly tacit.** So the same technology cuts the bottom of the labour market and lifts the top. Seniors get more productive, take on more work, and get paid more for the part of the job only they can still do.

- **The average hides a fork.** In jobs with no experience premium (data entry, ticket agents, fast-food cooks) wages are actually falling. In jobs with high experience premium (lawyers, insurance underwriters, marketing specialists) wages are rising.

## Two kinds of survivors
Here is my opinion: two kinds of people are going to own the next ten years.

The first is the generalist. Da Vinci was the extreme version: painter, anatomist, military engineer, hydrologist, urban planner, all at once. AI changes the maths completely. The generalist is back, in the form of the engineer who became a serious cancer researcher in her thirties, the lawyer who picked up data science and now advises governments on AI policy, the writer who learnt enough macroeconomics to be cited by working economists.

A generalist supercharged with AI in his pocket can pick up a new field on Tuesday and be useful in it by Friday. The cost of becoming competent in a new domain has fallen further in the last three years than it did in the entire previous century. The bottleneck previously was "did you put in your ten thousand hours." Now, it is "do you have the temperament to keep landing in new places and getting good at them quickly."

LinkedIn's 2025 Jobs on the Rise report has AI-integration and operations roles up 25% year on year, while pure specialist roles in copywriting, translation, and financial analysis are in decline. Roles that explicitly reward cross-domain fluency, like AI Product Manager and Chief of Staff, pay between $150k and $240k.

The second kind of winner is the super-specialist. The top one tenth of one percent inside any field. The surgeon doing the rare cases nobody else will touch. The lawyer carrying a Supreme Court argument every two years. The artist whose voice is recognisable in the first three seconds. AI is becoming a passable tenth-percentile worker in almost any field. It is not, yet, a passable top-of-field one. The work where the cost of being wrong is enormous, the work where the client is paying for a name and a decade of judgement, will keep paying humans well for a long time.

The middle is the part that gets eaten. The mid-tier specialist doing mid-tier specialist work was the meat of the white-collar economy for fifty years. That is the layer AI is most efficient at replacing, because the work is mostly codifiable and the worker has neither the breadth of the generalist nor the depth of the master.

If I were eighteen today, I would pick one of the two ends and run at it.

## How long any of this lasts
This is an interesting question. Slightly off-topic but late last year on Nikhil Kamath's [podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rni7Fz7208c), Elon Musk predicted that working would become optional inside ten to twenty years. People might still do it for fulfilment, the way some people grow vegetables, but not because they need to eat. A few months later, on Twitter, he made the policy version of it [explicit](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2044990537145753894):

![Elon Musk tweet from April 17, 2026: "Universal HIGH INCOME via checks issued by the Federal government is the best way to deal with unemployment caused by AI. AI/robotics will produce goods & services far in excess of the increase in the money supply, so there will not be inflation." The tweet has 69 million views.](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/UsamaEjaz0/thoughts-public/main/images/elon-uhi-twt.png)

When pushed on what that economy would actually feel like, by a follower asking who gets the penthouse if everyone has a billion dollars, he kept pointing at the same source:

![Twitter exchange. The first user asks: if abundance is so high that money means nothing, what determines who gets the penthouse, and how do we deal with day-to-day scarcity for things that cannot be mass manufactured by robots. Elon Musk replies that AI and robotics will mean everyone can have a penthouse if they want, that the output of goods and services will be several orders of magnitude higher than today's economy, and recommends "Read the Iain Banks Culture books for the best imagining of how it will be."](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/UsamaEjaz0/thoughts-public/main/images/elon-arg-uhi.png)

In Banks's books, almost everything is run by AIs called "Minds". The civilisation is called the Culture. There is no money, no scarcity, no need to work. People live three or four hundred years and do whatever they like with that time: art, science, sport, climbing mountains, learning dead languages. Some of them get bored and end up volunteering for dangerous assignments in less developed civilisations just to feel like something is at stake. That is the version of post-AI life Musk is pointing at.

Whether all of this actually arrives, on what timeline, and in what form, is another conversation. It is a vision worth thinking through properly, and that deserves its own post.

If you are still reading, pick a side. Narrow until you are unmistakably the best at one thing, or widen until you can land in any room and be useful inside a week. The middle is where the layoffs are happening. Things are moving so fast that you have to pick a direction now and course-correct as you go.
